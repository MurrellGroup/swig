# Glossary

Terms are grouped by topic. Each definition links to a relevant Wikipedia concept; chapter links lead to the fuller explanation in this guide. The official AIRR documentation is linked directly because its field definitions are specific to that standard.

## Recognition and immune cells

**Adaptive immunity.** Responses mediated by lymphocytes with diverse, individually specific receptors, including expansion of selected cells and development of memory. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptive_immune_system) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**Affinity.** Strength of one binding interaction between a binding site and its target, under specified conditions. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binding_affinity) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

**Antibody / immunoglobulin.** A heavy- and light-chain protein made by B-lineage cells. Its variable domains bind antigen; its constant domains determine other interactions and functions. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibody) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**Antigen.** Material recognized by an antibody or antigen receptor. T-cell recognition usually concerns a molecular fragment presented by another molecule. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigen) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**Antigen-presenting cell.** A cell that displays antigen-derived material to T cells. Dendritic cells, macrophages, and B cells have important professional antigen-presenting roles. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigen-presenting_cell) · [Chapter 4](04-t-cell-responses.md)

**Avidity.** Combined binding strength when several contacts can act together, influenced by affinity, geometry, and the number of binding sites. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avidity) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

**B cell.** A lymphocyte that expresses an immunoglobulin receptor and can generate antibody-secreting or memory descendants after activation. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_cell) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**B-cell receptor, BCR.** Membrane immunoglobulin together with associated signalling proteins. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-cell_receptor) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**CD4 / CD8.** T-cell co-receptors associated, in conventional alpha–beta T cells, with MHC class II and class I recognition respectively. [CD4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD4), [CD8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD8) · [Chapter 4](04-t-cell-responses.md)

**CDR.** Complementarity-determining region: one of three variable loops in each receptor variable domain. The heavy- and light-chain CDRs together help form an antibody's binding surface. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementarity-determining_region) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**Cross-reactivity.** Recognition of more than one molecular target by the same receptor or antibody. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-reactivity) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**Epitope.** The particular molecular feature recognized by a receptor or antibody. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epitope) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**Fc region.** The antibody constant-region portion that interacts with Fc receptors and other immune components. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragment_crystallizable_region) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**Framework region.** A variable-domain region between CDRs that contributes to domain structure and can affect receptor function. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framework_region) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**HLA.** Human leukocyte antigen: the human MHC system. HLA variation affects which peptides can be presented. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_leukocyte_antigen) · [Chapter 4](04-t-cell-responses.md)

**Innate immunity.** Defence processes using inherited recognition and effector systems, including barriers, inflammatory responses, and many myeloid-cell activities. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innate_immune_system) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**MHC.** Major histocompatibility complex; its class I and II proteins present peptides to conventional T cells. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_histocompatibility_complex) · [Chapter 4](04-t-cell-responses.md)

**Plasma cell.** A differentiated B-lineage cell specialized for antibody secretion. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_cell) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

**T cell.** A lymphocyte whose antigen receptor guides interactions with other cells or presented molecular targets. Its functions depend on its differentiated state. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T_cell) · [Chapter 4](04-t-cell-responses.md)

**T-cell receptor, TCR.** A receptor usually composed of alpha and beta chains, or gamma and delta chains, that signals through an associated CD3 complex. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-cell_receptor) · [Chapter 1](01-recognition-and-receptors.md)

**T follicular helper cell, Tfh.** A specialized helper T cell that supports B-cell responses in lymphoid follicles and germinal centres. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follicular_B_helper_T_cells) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

## Receptor assembly and B-cell diversification

**AID.** Activation-induced cytidine deaminase, an enzyme that initiates DNA lesions involved in SHM and class-switch recombination. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activation-induced_cytidine_deaminase) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

**Allele.** An inherited sequence version of a gene or other genomic region. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allele) · [Chapter 6](06-vdj-assignment.md)

**Allelic exclusion.** Regulation that usually restricts expression of a receptor-chain type to one of the two inherited alleles in a cell; its strength and exceptions depend on the locus. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allelic_exclusion) · [Chapter 2](02-vdj-recombination.md)

**Class switching.** DNA recombination that changes the antibody heavy-chain constant region while retaining the rearranged variable region. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoglobulin_class_switching) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

**Clonal expansion.** Increase in the number of cells belonging to a clone through proliferation. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clonal_selection) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

**Germinal centre.** A structure in a lymphoid follicle where activated B cells proliferate, diversify, and undergo selection with help from other cells. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germinal_center) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

**Germline reference.** A catalogue of inherited receptor segments used to interpret rearranged sequences. “Germline” distinguishes inherited sequence from the somatically rearranged or mutated receptor. [Germline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germline) · [Chapter 8](08-germline-variation.md)

**Isotype.** An antibody class defined by its heavy-chain constant region, such as IgM, IgD, IgG, IgA, or IgE. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotype_(immunology)) · [Chapter 3](03-b-cell-responses.md)

**Junction.** The sequence spanning joined receptor segments. In AIRR/IMGT usage, the reported junction includes conserved flanking residues around CDR3. [Junctional diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junctional_diversity), [AIRR definition](https://docs.airr-community.org/en/stable/datarep/rearrangements.html) · [Chapter 2](02-vdj-recombination.md)

**Locus.** A genomic location or region; receptor loci contain the segments for particular chain types. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locus_(genetics)) · [Chapter 2](02-vdj-recombination.md)

**Memory cell.** A persistent lymphocyte state contributing to responses after a previous antigen encounter; B- and T-cell memory comprise several cell populations. [Memory B cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_B_cell), [memory T cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memory_T_cell) · [Chapters 3](03-b-cell-responses.md) and [4](04-t-cell-responses.md)

**N addition.** Addition of nucleotides at a rearrangement junction without copying an inherited segment template. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junctional_diversity) · [Chapter 2](02-vdj-recombination.md)

**Productive rearrangement.** A rearranged sequence meeting the specified criteria for an intact receptor coding sequence, including reading-frame and stop-codon checks. [V(D)J recombination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V(D)J_recombination), [AIRR specification](https://docs.airr-community.org/en/stable/datarep/rearrangements.html) · [Chapter 6](06-vdj-assignment.md)

**RAG1 / RAG2.** Proteins that recognize recombination signal sequences and initiate receptor-segment cutting during V(D)J recombination. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recombination-activating_gene) · [Chapter 2](02-vdj-recombination.md)

**Repertoire.** The collection of receptor sequences, or receptor-bearing cells, present in a specified biological population or sample. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_repertoire) · [Chapter 9](09-repertoires-and-clones.md)

**SHM.** Somatic hypermutation: acquired diversification of immunoglobulin variable-region DNA in activated B cells. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_hypermutation) · [Chapters 3](03-b-cell-responses.md) and [10](10-shm-alignments-and-trees.md)

**Synonymous / replacement change.** A coding-sequence change that preserves / alters the encoded amino acid. [Synonymous substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synonymous_substitution), [nonsynonymous substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonsynonymous_substitution) · [Chapter 10](10-shm-alignments-and-trees.md)

**Tolerance.** Processes that limit damaging immune responses to self or other tolerated material, including receptor editing, deletion, anergy, and regulation. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_tolerance) · [Chapters 3](03-b-cell-responses.md) and [4](04-t-cell-responses.md)

**V(D)J recombination.** Somatic DNA rearrangement that joins variable, optional diversity, and joining segments to assemble an antigen-receptor variable region. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V(D)J_recombination) · [Chapter 2](02-vdj-recombination.md)

## Sequencing and family inference

**AIRR.** Adaptive immune receptor repertoire; also used for the community standards and formats for describing these data. [Official standards](https://docs.airr-community.org/) · [Chapter 5](05-samples-sequences-and-counts.md)

**Cell barcode.** A sequence label used to associate observations with the same captured cell, subject to the assay's capture and barcode errors. [Single-cell sequencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-cell_sequencing) · [Chapter 5](05-samples-sequences-and-counts.md)

**Chimera.** A molecule combining sequence from different sources. A PCR chimera can form when amplification switches between templates. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimera_(molecular_biology)) · [Chapter 7](07-errors-and-chimeras.md)

**Clone / clonal family.** Descendants of a common cell. A sequence-inferred family is an estimate based on observed receptor evidence and a grouping rule. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clone_(cell_biology)) · [Chapter 9](09-repertoires-and-clones.md)

**Clonotype.** A receptor-defined category of cells or sequences; its precise chain, sequence, and gene-call definition should be stated. [Immune repertoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_repertoire) · [Chapter 9](09-repertoires-and-clones.md)

**Denoising.** Correction or grouping of sequence variants judged compatible with measurement error under a chosen model. [Error correction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Error_detection_and_correction) · [Chapter 7](07-errors-and-chimeras.md)

**Edit distance.** A measure of sequence difference allowing substitutions, insertions, and deletions. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levenshtein_distance) · [Chapter 9](09-repertoires-and-clones.md)

**Hamming distance.** The number of mismatching positions in two equal-length strings. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamming_distance) · [Chapter 9](09-repertoires-and-clones.md)

**Paired-chain data.** Evidence linking the two receptor-chain sequences expressed by the same cell, such as antibody heavy–light or TCR alpha–beta pairs. [Single-cell sequencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-cell_sequencing) · [Chapter 5](05-samples-sequences-and-counts.md)

**Phred quality.** A logarithmic measure of estimated base-call error probability: Q20 corresponds to 1%, and Q30 to 0.1%. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phred_quality_score) · [Chapter 5](05-samples-sequences-and-counts.md)

**Read.** A sequence reported from one sequencing observation, which can contain errors and may derive from an amplified copy. [DNA sequencing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequencing) · [Chapter 5](05-samples-sequences-and-counts.md)

**Single linkage.** Clustering by paths of qualifying pairwise similarities; endpoints of a long path can be relatively dissimilar. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-linkage_clustering) · [Chapter 9](09-repertoires-and-clones.md)

**UMI.** Unique molecular identifier: a sequence tag intended to identify an original captured molecule before amplification. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unique_molecular_identifier) · [Chapter 5](05-samples-sequences-and-counts.md)

## Trees, uncertainty, and experiments

**Alignment.** An arrangement of sequences into columns representing proposed corresponding positions. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequence_alignment) · [Chapter 10](10-shm-alignments-and-trees.md)

**Branch length.** An amount of inferred evolutionary change, often substitutions per site, along a tree edge. [Phylogenetic tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetic_tree) · [Chapter 10](10-shm-alignments-and-trees.md)

**Consensus sequence.** A position-by-position representative of a collection or probability distribution. Its construction should specify how each position is chosen. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_sequence) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)

**Hidden Markov model, HMM.** A model in which unobserved states progress along a sequence and generate observed characters or their probabilities. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Markov_model) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)

**Immunogen.** Material capable of eliciting an immune response in a particular setting. [Immunogenicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunogenicity) · [Chapter 12](12-vaccine-design.md)

**Likelihood.** The probability assigned to the observed data as a function of a proposed model or ancestral explanation. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likelihood_function) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)

**Marginalization.** Summing or integrating over alternative values of variables to obtain a distribution for the quantity of interest. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_distribution) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)

**MCMC.** Markov chain Monte Carlo: a family of methods for sampling a probability distribution through dependent random updates. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markov_chain_Monte_Carlo) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)

**MRCA.** Most recent common ancestor of a specified set of descendants. A sampled B-cell family's MRCA can already be somatically mutated. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_recent_common_ancestor) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)

**Neutralization.** Reduction of infectivity or activity in a defined functional assay, for example antibody-mediated inhibition of viral entry. [Neutralizing antibody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutralizing_antibody) · [Chapter 12](12-vaccine-design.md)

**Parsimony.** An inference criterion favouring explanations requiring the fewest changes under specified rules. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_parsimony_(phylogenetics)) · [Chapter 10](10-shm-alignments-and-trees.md)

**Phylogeny.** A reconstruction of relationships through common ancestry. [Phylogenetic tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetic_tree) · [Chapter 10](10-shm-alignments-and-trees.md)

**Posterior probability.** Probability after combining the observations with a prior and likelihood under a specified model. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posterior_probability) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)

**Prior probability.** Probability assigned before incorporating the particular observations used in an inference. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_probability) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)

**UCA.** Unmutated common ancestor: the reconstructed pre-SHM, already-rearranged starting sequence of a B-cell receptor family. [Ancestral sequence reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancestral_sequence_reconstruction) · [Chapter 11](11-unmutated-common-ancestors.md)
