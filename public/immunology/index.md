# B- and T-cell immunology

B and T cells recognize molecular features of the world around them using proteins called antigen receptors. Each cell carries a particular receptor, and different cells carry different ones. During an immune response, cells whose receptors recognize a relevant target can multiply. Their descendants help control infection and can contribute to immune memory.

Receptor sequences carry traces of this history. They record the joining of inherited gene segments during cell development. In B cells, they can also record mutations acquired during an immune response. Comparing sequences can therefore tell us something about how receptors were assembled, which cells may share an ancestor, and how an antibody family changed over time.

This guide develops the biology behind those comparisons. The examples mainly concern human and mouse immune systems. Other species can differ in receptor organization, diversification, and antibody classes.
